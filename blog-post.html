What is an ATS (Applicant Tracking System)?

An ATS is software used by employers to collect, filter, and rank job applications automatically. When you submit your resume online, it goes straight into this system — not to a human.

The ATS scans your resume for specific keywords, skills, and qualifications that match the job description. If your resume doesn't score high enough, it gets filtered out automatically — no matter how qualified you actually are.

💡 Think of ATS as a keyword-matching machine. If the job description says "Project Management" and your resume says "managed projects", the ATS may not make that connection — and reject you.

How ATS Ranks Your Resume

Every ATS works slightly differently, but most use a similar scoring system:

  • Keyword matching — Does your resume contain the exact words from the job description?
  • Skills matching — Do you have the required technical and soft skills?
  • Experience relevance — Does your work history match the role?
  • Education requirements — Do you meet the degree or certification requirements?
  • Formatting — Can the ATS actually parse and read your resume correctly?

7 Ways to Beat ATS in 2026

1. Mirror the Job Description Language

This is the single most important thing you can do. Read the job description carefully and use the exact same words and phrases in your resume. If they say "cross-functional collaboration", use that exact phrase — not "worked with different teams".

✅ Pro Tip:

Copy the job description into a word frequency tool to find the most repeated keywords. These are your highest priority terms to include.

2. Use a Clean, Simple Format

Fancy resume templates with columns, graphics, tables, and text boxes look great to humans but confuse ATS parsers. Stick to a simple single-column format with clear section headers.

❌ Avoid These ATS Killers:

Headers and footers, text boxes, tables, images, graphics, fancy fonts, and PDF files from design tools like Canva or Adobe.

3. Use Standard Section Headers

ATS systems look for standard section names. Use these exact headers:

  • WORK EXPERIENCE or EXPERIENCE
  • EDUCATION
  • SKILLS
  • SUMMARY or PROFESSIONAL SUMMARY
  • CERTIFICATIONS

4. Include Both Acronyms and Full Forms

Some ATS systems search for acronyms, others for full terms. Play it safe by including both. For example: "Search Engine Optimization (SEO)" or "Artificial Intelligence (AI)".

5. Quantify Your Achievements

Numbers stand out to both ATS and human reviewers. Instead of "improved website performance", write "improved website load time by 45%, increasing conversions by 20%".

6. Tailor Every Application

One resume does not fit all jobs. Each job posting has different keywords and priorities. A tailored resume that matches 80% of the job description will always outperform a generic resume that matches 40%.

7. Submit in the Right Format

When in doubt, submit your resume as a .docx or .pdf file. Most modern ATS systems handle both, but .docx is the safest choice. Avoid .jpg, .png, or design tool exports.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does every company use ATS?

98% of Fortune 500 companies and most mid-size companies use ATS. Small startups may review resumes manually, but it's always safer to optimize for ATS.

Will ATS optimization make my resume look unnatural?

Not if done correctly. Good ATS optimization means naturally incorporating relevant keywords into your existing experience — not keyword stuffing.

Should I use a different resume for every job?

Yes, ideally. Tailoring your resume to each job description significantly increases your ATS score. Tools like ResumeMatch AI make this process fast and easy.

What ATS score should I aim for?

Aim for 70% or higher. A score above 80% puts you in an excellent position to be shortlisted by recruiters.

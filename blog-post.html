1. What is an ATS and How Does it Work?

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is software that companies use to collect, sort, and filter job applications at scale. When you apply online, your resume goes directly into an ATS — not into a recruiter's inbox.

The ATS does several things automatically:

  • Parses your resume — extracts your name, contact info, work history, education, and skills into a structured database
  • Scores your resume — compares your content against the job description using keyword matching algorithms
  • Ranks all applicants — sorts candidates by score so recruiters see the best matches first
  • Filters out low scorers — resumes below a threshold score are automatically archived and never seen

💡 The ATS doesn't care how impressive your experience is. It only cares whether the right keywords appear in the right places. A junior candidate with a well-optimized resume will outrank a senior candidate with a poorly formatted one.

Popular ATS platforms used by companies include Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, Taleo, iCIMS, and SAP SuccessFactors. Each has slightly different algorithms, but keyword matching is universal across all of them.

2. Why Your Resume is Getting Rejected

Most candidates assume rejection means they're underqualified. In reality, the most common reason for ATS rejection has nothing to do with qualifications. Here are the top reasons:

Wrong or missing keywords

If the job description says "stakeholder management" and your resume says "client communication", the ATS may score those as a mismatch — even though they mean the same thing. ATS systems are not smart enough to understand synonyms reliably. You need to use the exact same language as the job description.

Unreadable formatting

Tables, columns, text boxes, headers/footers, and graphics confuse ATS parsers. The software tries to read your resume left-to-right, top-to-bottom as plain text. Anything that breaks this flow can cause important information to be misread or lost entirely.

Wrong file format

Many ATS systems struggle to parse .docx files with complex formatting. PDFs can be even worse — some ATS platforms cannot read PDFs at all. Unless the job posting specifically requests a PDF, submit a clean .docx file.

Missing section headers

ATS systems look for standard section headers like "Work Experience", "Education", "Skills". If you get creative with headers like "My Journey" or "Where I've Been", the ATS may not know how to categorize that content.

⚠️ A beautifully designed resume with columns, icons, and graphics may look impressive to humans — but it can score 0% on ATS because the parser cannot read it correctly.

3. The Keyword Matching Problem

This is the most important thing to understand about ATS optimization. Keywords are everything.

When a recruiter creates a job posting, they use specific language — often copied directly from internal job frameworks or industry standards. The ATS is configured to look for those exact terms. Your job is to mirror that language as closely as possible.

4. Formatting Mistakes That Kill ATS Scores

Even a perfectly keyword-matched resume can fail if it's formatted incorrectly. Here are the most common formatting mistakes:

Using tables or columns

Multi-column layouts look clean to humans but ATS parsers read left-to-right across the entire page. A two-column resume gets read as a jumbled mix of text from both columns — making your experience section incomprehensible to the software.

Text inside images or graphics

Skill bars, progress circles, infographic-style elements — none of this text can be read by ATS. If your skills section is a visual graphic, the ATS sees nothing. Write all skills as plain text.

Non-standard fonts

Stick to ATS-safe fonts: Calibri, Arial, Garamond, Georgia, Times New Roman, or Helvetica. Decorative fonts may not render correctly and can cause parsing errors.

Headers and footers

Many ATS systems cannot read text placed in the header or footer section of a Word document. Never put your contact information in the document header — place it in the main body of the document.

Fancy bullet points

Custom bullet symbols (arrows, squares, checkmarks) can sometimes cause parsing issues. Use standard round bullets (•) or hyphens (-) to be safe.

5. How to Optimize Your Resume Step by Step

Step 1: Start with the job description

Before editing your resume, highlight every skill, qualification, and responsibility mentioned in the job description. These are your target keywords.

Step 2: Match your language exactly

Go through your resume and replace your phrasing with the exact language from the job description wherever it's accurate. Don't exaggerate — but don't undersell yourself with vague language when the JD uses specific terms.

Step 3: Fix your formatting

Convert to a single-column layout. Remove tables, text boxes, and graphics. Use standard section headers. Make sure contact info is in the body, not the header.

Step 4: Add a skills section

A dedicated skills section gives the ATS a clear, scannable list of your capabilities. Include both hard skills (tools, technologies, certifications) and relevant soft skills that appear in the job description.

Step 5: Quantify your achievements

Numbers make your resume both ATS-friendly (specific terms match better) and human-friendly (concrete results are more compelling). "Increased sales by 34%" is better than "improved sales performance".

6. ATS in India — What's Different

Job seekers in India face unique challenges with ATS. Here's what you need to know:

Indian job portals use ATS too

Naukri, LinkedIn, Indeed India, Internshala, and Shine all use ATS-style filtering. When you upload your resume to Naukri, it's parsed and indexed — recruiters search for specific keywords and your profile's visibility depends entirely on how well it's optimized.

Campus placement ATS

Large companies visiting campuses for placements — TCS, Infosys, Wipro, Accenture, Deloitte, Goldman Sachs — all use ATS to screen the thousands of applications they receive during placement season. Your CGPA alone won't save you if your resume isn't keyword-optimized.

Keywords differ by sector

Indian IT companies look for specific certifications (AWS, Azure, GCP), specific tech stacks, and project experience. Consulting firms look for structured problem-solving language. Finance roles require specific tool knowledge (Bloomberg, SAP, Tally). Tailor your keywords to the sector.

English language matters

Indian ATS systems — especially those from global MNCs operating in India — are calibrated for professional English. Avoid regional language terms or abbreviations that may not be in the ATS keyword database.

8. Frequently Asked Questions

Does every company use ATS?

98% of Fortune 500 companies do. Mid-size companies (100+ employees) increasingly use ATS too. Startups and small businesses are less likely to use formal ATS, but may still use LinkedIn or Naukri's built-in filtering. Assume ATS is in play unless you're applying directly to a small startup.

Should I use a different resume for every job?

Yes — ideally. At minimum, update your skills section and summary paragraph for each role. The more tailored your resume is to a specific job description, the higher your ATS score. ATSCheckPro makes this fast — paste a new JD and get a freshly optimized resume in seconds.

Will keyword stuffing help my ATS score?

No. Stuffing keywords randomly or hiding white text on a white background used to work years ago. Modern ATS systems detect this. More importantly, once your resume reaches a human, keyword-stuffed resumes look terrible and get rejected immediately. Integrate keywords naturally.

What ATS score should I aim for?

A score of 75% or above significantly increases your chances of passing the ATS filter. 85%+ puts you in the top tier of applicants. Below 60% and you're unlikely to get through. ATSCheckPro shows you your exact score and what's needed to improve it.

Does a good ATS score guarantee an interview?

No — but a bad ATS score almost guarantees rejection. A high ATS score gets your resume in front of a human recruiter. From there, the quality of your experience and communication skills take over. Think of ATS optimization as getting through the door — what happens after is up to you.
